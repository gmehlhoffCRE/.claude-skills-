Change Management
Effectively communicating new space use concepts to your employees to ensure program success.

At Cresa, we believe in the importance of employee engagement and the value it can create for your business. When an organization’s employees are not prepared to make a transition, the effectiveness of your new workplace program is in jeopardy. With this in mind, we’re committed not only to enhancing your operations, but also to guiding your people through program implementation. We provide the tools and support necessary to prepare your team to embrace new concepts and successfully adapt to workplace changes.

We begin by identifying potential points of resistance and exploring important questions:
Should change be incremental or all at once?
Will employee reactions vary depending on work style or job type?
How can we manage expectations during the transition?
What tools or trainings are necessary to empower employees to make the change?

An organization’s willingness to adapt is the greatest indicator of its ability to succeed. Our team can ensure your transition is carefully planned and thoughtfully executed with minimal disruption to ongoing operations.

Tenets of a smooth transition
Communicate
Gaining the support of your employees requires transparency about what – and why – things are changing. We develop a Communication Plan that conveys the case for change and your vision for the future.

Engage
Sharing the Implementation Plan & Timeline with employees is crucial because it minimizes uncertainty. This allows employees to become active participants in the process, not passive observers.

Implement
Having been trained on the new processes and tools, employees are empowered to adapt and thrive during the transition.

Assess and Adapt
Measuring the effectiveness of the program is key. The resulting data serves to highlight improvements and indicate any opportunities to refine the program.

Lease Audit & Enforcement
Conducting a thorough review of your portfolio to generate savings and eliminate risk.

Acting as your advocate, we’ll review past and current billing practices to ensure compliance and reveal savings. 

Lease Audits provide an excellent opportunity to reduce operating costs, recover missed savings opportunities and protect your interests long-term. While mistakes made by landlords are not intentional, they are inevitable. Working with – and not in opposition to – your landlord, our advisors initiate an inquiry on basic lease compliance and fairness. 

The right audit for you 
There are several levels of review which can be conducted individually or together. Often the size of the location and the potential for recovery determines the type of audit required. 

A Desktop Review is applicable to all leases and involves an accounts payable review of the expense billing against traditional account processing standards. We review back-up documentation for adequacy and conduct a quantitative verification of the calculation to ensure it’s consistent with the negotiated terms. This kind of audit is typically performed on operating expenses but may also be advisable for a 12-month history if there is uncertainty over past payment practices. 

A Desktop Analysis is a qualitative review that involves the investigation of suspicious charges. We provide an opinion of the correct recalculation and suggest next steps for correcting errors or negotiating reductions in billing. This type of audit can be conducted without being on-site. 

A Forensic Audit invokes the “audit clause” of your lease and may or may not require on-site work. The use of a third-party CPA firm may be required depending on the lease requirements. 

Audits are an investment, not an expense 
Experience shows that over 30% of our audits uncover savings for our clients due to errors in landlord billing. Our goal is to achieve cash recovery and cost avoidance for you while eliminating potential risk from your lease terms.

Incorrectly calculated or recorded base year

Calendar v. fiscal year lease language 
Pass-through operating expenses excluded in lease 
Changes in occupier’s proportionate share 
Improper amortization of improvements 
Aggregated v. separated expenses

Market Survey Book. First, we produce a comprehensive Market Survey Book, outlining all available options in the desired location. Market Tour Book. Following your review of the Market Survey Book, we’ll work with you to make a shortlist of the sites you would like to tour. The Market Tour book will serve as a helpful guide on these tours, offering key information to keep in mind as you contemplate your options. 
Site Tours. Together, we’ll tour each shortlisted site highlighted in the Market Tour Book.
Feasibility Planning

Landlord RFPs

Financial Analysis
Qualitative and Financial Analysis. After confirming all landlord responses to the RFP have been received, our team conducts a thorough Qualitative and Financial Analysis of each location. Prepared in a matrix format, we utilize several financial analysis platforms with customizable features to ensure all responses are being assessed with your specific requirements in mind. We complete all analyses on a cash and GAAP basis, encompassing the new FASB/IASB regulations. Additionally, we work with the landlord, architect, project manager and engineer (if applicable) to collect base building structural and systems drawings, USF/RSF, add-on factors and pro-rata share.
Our analysis also includes an evaluation of the landlord, property management services, workout situations, reasonableness, and flexibility on lease negotiations. The test-fit sizing and cost estimates are included in the analysis allowing you to have a full cost comparison.
Building Inspections. We recommend that a detailed building system inspection report be completed. This is critical for larger NNN situations where you will be responsible for maintaining and repairing building systems. This report is used to leverage negotiations with the landlord and to have any deficiencies addressed prior to the lease commencement.

Counter Proposals. To your satisfaction, we’ll offer terms of a counter proposal(s) to the landlord on your behalf.

Negotiation of Terms. We negotiate a detailed, non-binding LOI that sets forth the preliminary terms and conditions of the proposed transaction. This same document is submitted to you for review and approval by an Authorized Signatory. Additionally, we deliver the approved LOI to your legal counsel as directed by stakeholders, for their review.

Maximizing Recovery & Minimizing Time on Market     
We believe in seizing the first opportunity to sublease your space. It’s the only surefire way to maximize recovery and minimize your space’s time on the market. Though this approach may seem overly simple, the math shows that each sublease property has a finite period in which to achieve recovery. After this point, as months pass, the only way to maximize recovery is by securing a higher rent – often easier said than done. To facilitate the sublease of your space, you should follow the guiding principles outlined below.

Be realistic. Use the rental rates of competitive properties, recent transactions and market absorption to create a realistic timeline for achievable recovery.  
Position the space appropriately.For a large sublease like an entire building, we suggest you hire a property manager to manage the sublease assets, but assume responsibility for supporting subtenant needs, collecting rents, paying bills and manage repairs. 
Partial subleases are more desirable when they offer a good mix of what space users are seeking such as a lobby presence. These subleases should function efficiently as a true standalone office in order maximize market interest.  
Short-term subleases (less than 3 years) are best suited to a “plug and play solution” which enables a quick and seamless start-up. Your target market will be looking for a fully furnished space inclusive of AV equipment, café appliances and network and phone connectivity. This ensures your future tenants experience little to no downtime as a result of their move into your space.  
Be proactive. Work with your outside counsel early on to have a standard sublease in place along with a consent agreement form that is acceptable to both you and your landlord. Agreeing with your landlord on these terms in advance means no lost time – or tenants! – when an opportunity to sublet arises. 
Incentivize. Plan to offer full market commissions to cooperating brokers who bring potential tenants through your space. Remember that these brokers likely represent 90% of total tenants in the market.
Clean up. Would you want to rent your space? Ensure your space is impeccable with nothing in disrepair. It needs to look like a showroom so repaint the walls, clean the carpets and check that all heat and A/C units are functioning.
Get creative. Think about how you can be competitive in your market or “sweeten the deal.” For larger spaces, ask the Property Manager to assist with showings so they can answer technical questions. Offer to complete test-fit plans or fund tenant improvements or renovations. Inquire about the possibility of a landlord buyout. 
Be decisive. When an opportunity presents itself, you need to act decisively. Being knowledgeable about the process and your options will leave you confident in your decisions.
