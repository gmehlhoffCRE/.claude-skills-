## The core argument (occupier-only = conflict-free)

Cresa's model rests on a simple principle: **because we do not represent landlords, our
advisors sit on one side of the table — the occupier's.** That structure eliminates the
conflict of interest baked into traditional brokerage and lets Cresa focus on helping
tenants make informed decisions and achieve the best possible outcomes.

## The dual-agency problem (how to frame the risk)

- **Dual agency = incompatible incentives.** When a broker represents both landlords and
  tenants, the interests collide: landlords (to whom the broker owes a fiduciary duty)
  want the highest rates and fewest concessions; tenants want the opposite. "Something
  must give, and the advantage typically goes to the landlord."
- **Steering.** Brokers have an obligation to bring tenants to their own company's
  listings — so the incentive is to steer tenants toward in-house listings over a better
  competitor option. Not in the tenant's best interest.
- **Follow the money.** Based on public filings of large full-service CRE firms, roughly
  **80–90% of their revenue comes from representing landlords** (leasing, property
  management, investment sales). The contest for a dual-agency broker's attention is no
  contest.
- **They disclose it themselves.** The largest dual-agency firms publish standard
  conflict-of-interest disclaimers in their filings — e.g., diverse business lines and a
  broad client base subject them to "numerous potential, actual or perceived conflicts of
  interest," and the "primary duty of loyalty we owe to one client may potentially be
  weakened or compromised by a relationship we also maintain with another client or third
  party."
- **Rising scrutiny.** High-profile lawsuits over dual agency have been filed in recent
  years; CFOs who wouldn't accept this conflict from other service providers are applying
  the same due diligence to their real estate relationships.

## Data usage & protection (the modern angle)

As CRE becomes data-driven, information handling is a critical tenant issue — two-sided:
1. **Is market data used to get the tenant a better outcome?** Used responsibly, data
   creates leverage by generating competitive tension between viable options. Value comes
   from applying it strategically in the tenant's interest — not from the data alone.
2. **Is the tenant's own confidential information protected?** Rental rates, renewal
   timing, expansion/contraction plans, workforce strategy, and location preferences can
   all be used to weaken a tenant's leverage before negotiations begin.

**Cresa Core** supports this: it connects real estate data, portfolio insights, and
decision-making tools in one platform with **enterprise-grade controls that protect client
data and keep it governed end-to-end.** (See `cresa-core.md`.)

## A Tenant's Checklist for Advisor Transparency (reusable)

Five questions a company should ask any CRE advisor — useful as a pitch/RFP sidebar or
client-education asset:

1. **What is the advisor's track record with similar clients and transactions?** Get
   references; research similar deals; have conflicts arisen before; did tenant clients
   feel they got their money's worth and come back?
2. **Does the advisor or firm represent landlords?** Raise conflicts early — ask whether
   the firm represents landlords, manages properties, holds current listings, or has
   asset/property-management agreements with companies that could sit across the table.
3. **How will confidential information be protected?** Understand how rental rates, lease
   info, renewal timing, occupancy costs, expansion/contraction plans, and workforce
   strategy are collected, stored, shared, and protected.
4. **How are fees, commissions, or incentives structured?** Insist on full disclosure —
   including fees/commissions landlords pay them, plus bonuses or discounts.
5. **How does the advisor create leverage?** They should be able to explain how they
   compare alternatives, create competitive tension, pressure-test landlord proposals, and
   negotiate terms that support the tenant's business goals.

> For Cresa, every answer favors the client: no landlord relationships, one side of the
> table, transparent fees, governed data, and leverage created through genuine competition.
