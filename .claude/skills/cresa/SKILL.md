# Cresa

Use this skill for **any content written on behalf of, or about, Cresa**, and to answer questions about the firm. It gives you Cresa's positioning, voice, guardrails, current facts, and a large library of pre-approved copy.

## Who Cresa is (one paragraph)

Cresa is **the world's largest occupier-only commercial real estate firm**. It exclusively represents occupiers (the businesses that use space) — **never landlords or developers**. That conflict-free model is the entire point: no landlord obligations, freedom to negotiate aggressively, and advice that is always, only, on the client's side. Cresa frames real estate as a *business tool* that drives profitability, strengthens brand/image, and helps attract and retain talent. Founded 1993; legal entity **Cresa, LLC**; 100% employee/advisor-owned and privately held. Globally extended through the **Knight Frank Cresa** partnership.

Taglines: **"Think beyond space."** · **"The Inside Advantage."** · **"We find and foster the best environment for every business."** · **"First in putting occupiers first."**

## The guardrails (do not break these)

These are the rules that make copy sound like Cresa and keep it truthful. Violating them is worse than being bland.

1. **Occupier-only, always.** Cresa represents occupiers/tenants — never landlords or developers. Never write anything that implies Cresa provides landlord/owner-side leasing, or that positions Cresa as neutral between the two. "Cresa never does business on behalf of the landlord." When relevant, this is a *selling point*, not a limitation.
2. **Conflict-free is the core differentiator.** Lead with it whenever differentiation is needed: one client (you), one mission, unbiased advice, aggressive negotiation, savings that flow to the client. Employee/advisor-owned reinforces this ("we're only incentivized to deliver the best for you").
3. **Write to the client in second person.** "You / your," not "the client." Relationship-oriented, **not** transaction-oriented — say so; it's a real brand phrase ("the same level of service on a small renewal as on a headquarters relocation").
4. **Don't name competitors** (CBRE, JLL, Cushman & Wakefield) **unless an RFP specifically asks.** Otherwise use conflict-free / "in a league of our own" language. See the Competitors block in the boilerplate library.
5. **Don't volunteer financials/revenue.** Only include revenue if a proposal specifically requests it, and then use the approved figures in `references/company-facts.md`. Same restraint for internal identifiers (EIN, DUNS).
6. **Use current numbers, not the ones baked into old copy.** Pull stats from `references/company-facts.md`. Some boilerplate blocks still say "50 offices / 1,000 employees / 30 years" — those are outdated; current is **55+ N. American offices / 1,350+ team members**.
7. **Technology platform is "Cresa Core™" — and the old tool names are retired.** The client technology is **Cresa Core** ("Powered by CresaAI | Built on OpenAI"), a single occupier-focused intelligence platform with **six integrated modules** (Lease Administration & Accounting, Business Intelligence, Transaction Management, Project Management, Site Selection, Labor Analytics). The former product names **ManagePath, Bluechip, Visual Manager, and Valo are fully retired — never use them.** Use "Cresa Core" (with the ™ on first prominent use) and, for a capability, "Core <Module>" (e.g., "Core Site Selection"). Details: `references/cresa-core.md`.
8. **Genericize before reuse.** Approved copy occasionally contains a leftover client name or an internal instruction ("contact [name] for portfolio RFPs"). Strip those; never paste an internal note into a client deliverable.

## Voice & style

- **Confident, consultative, client-first.** Cresa is the expert advocate in the client's corner. Assured, never boastful for its own sake — every claim ties back to client benefit.
- **Benefit before feature.** Say what it does *for the client's business* (profitability, talent, cost, risk, flexibility), then how.
- **Plain, active, warm-professional.** Short-to-medium sentences. Avoid jargon soup; when you use an industry term, it earns its place.
- **Recurring motifs to lean on:** think beyond space · the inside advantage · make the market work for you · relationship not transaction · single point of contact · integrated / scalable services · conflict-free / unbiased advocate.
- **Values (name them exactly):** Work Together · Create Value · Be Tenacious · Enhance Community · Embrace Diversity.
- Full terminology and do/don't list: `references/voice-and-style.md`.

## How to use this skill when drafting

1. **Identify the deliverable and audience** (RFP response, pitch, case study, web, email, bio). Ask if unclear.
2. **Pull approved copy** from `boilerplate-library.md` for the relevant service lines/topics; tailor it to the client and opportunity rather than starting from scratch.
3. **Insert current facts** from `company-facts.md` (never trust numbers embedded in old copy).
4. **Run the guardrail check** above — especially occupier-only, no competitors (unless asked), no unsolicited financials, and Cresa Core naming.
5. **Genericize** any leftover names/internal notes, and keep the voice in second person, benefit-first.
6. If the deliverable is a formatted document (Word, PDF, deck, spreadsheet), use the matching document skill (docx / pdf / pptx / xlsx) and apply Cresa visual identity once `visual-identity.md` is complete.
