# Cresa — Voice & Style

How Cresa sounds, the words it uses, and the lines it won't cross. Pair this with the guardrails in `SKILL.md`.

## Voice in one line

**The expert advocate in the client's corner** — confident, consultative, warm-professional, and always client-first. Cresa is never neutral; it is unapologetically on the occupier's side.

## Core principles

1. **Write to "you."** Address the client directly in second person. Reserve "we/our" for Cresa. Avoid "the client" / "the tenant" in client-facing copy.
2. **Benefit before feature.** Lead with the business outcome (profitability, talent, cost, risk, flexibility, control), then explain the how.
3. **Relationship, not transaction.** State it. Cresa gives "the same level of service on a small renewal as on a headquarters relocation."
4. **Plain and active.** Short-to-medium sentences, strong verbs, minimal jargon. Industry terms only when they carry real meaning.
5. **Confident, not boastful.** Claims are fine when tied to client benefit or backed by a fact (e.g., #1 in Tenant Advocacy). Avoid empty superlatives.
6. **Integrated and scalable.** Emphasize one point of contact, connected service lines, and services that flex with the client's portfolio.

## Signature phrases (use naturally, don't overuse)

- Think beyond space · The Inside Advantage · First in putting occupiers first
- Make the market work for you, not the other way around
- Unbiased advocate · conflict-free · in a league of our own · one customer: you
- Relationship-oriented, not transaction-oriented
- Single point of contact · integrated, scalable services · we connect the dots so you don't have to
- Occupier-only / occupier-focused (Cresa's defining descriptor)

## Terminology

| Prefer | Over / notes |
|---|---|
| **occupier** (positioning: occupier-only, occupier-focused) | "tenant" as a label for the client, except where it's the named service ("Tenant Representation") |
| **you / your** | "the client," "the tenant," "the customer" (in client-facing copy) |
| **Cresa Core™** and **Core <Module>** | ManagePath, Bluechip, Visual Manager, **Valo** — all RETIRED, never use |
| **Cresa, LLC** (legal name) | Cresa Global / Cresa Partners (only in explicit entity-history context) |
| **Project & Development Services** | mixing up the exact service-line names — use the canonical list in `company-facts.md` |
| **Knight Frank Cresa** (partnership) | "our overseas office" — it's a partnership working as an extension of the Cresa team |

## Capitalization / formatting

- Capitalize service-line and program names: Transaction Management, Portfolio Solutions, Project & Development Services, Lease Administration, Workplace Solutions, Location Advisory, Capital Strategies, Strategic Consulting; **Cresa Cares**, **Diversity, Inclusion & Belonging (DIB)**, **Employee Resource Groups (ERGs)**, **Quarterly Business Reviews (QBRs)**, **Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)**.
- Trademarks: **Cresa Core™** on first prominent use; **iSiteSelection™** where it appears.
- Values are proper phrases: **Work Together · Create Value · Be Tenacious · Enhance Community · Embrace Diversity.**
- Numbers as they appear in `company-facts.md` (e.g., "101M+ SF," "$7.8 Billion," "2,500+").

## Do / Don't

**Do**
- Lead differentiation with the conflict-free, occupier-only model.
- Tailor approved boilerplate to the specific client and opportunity.
- Use current stats from `company-facts.md`.
- Keep the client as the hero; Cresa is the guide.

**Don't**
- Imply Cresa represents, or is neutral toward, landlords/developers. Ever.
- Name competitors (CBRE, JLL, Cushman & Wakefield) unless an RFP explicitly asks.
- Volunteer revenue/financials or internal identifiers (EIN, DUNS) unless a proposal requires them.
- Use retired tool names (Valo, etc.) or outdated counts (50 offices / 1,000 employees).
- Paste internal usage notes, reviewer names, or a leftover client name from boilerplate into a deliverable.
- Over-sprinkle taglines — one strong motif per section beats a pile of slogans.

## A quick before/after

- ❌ "Cresa is a full-service real estate firm that works with tenants and landlords to close deals."
- ✅ "As the world's largest occupier-only firm, Cresa represents you — never the landlord — so every negotiation is run entirely in your interest."
